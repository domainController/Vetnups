What "vetted" actually means

Sample manifest — 7 of 30+ checks

"Vetted" comes from having a vet examine a horse before a sale. Racehorses and breeding stock were worth a fortune, and their condition wasn't always visible — so a qualified expert checked, not just the paperwork.

By 1901, the word had generalized: to subject something to careful examination. Today it's the standard term for scrutiny before a binding commitment — a hire, a loan, a visa, a signed contract.

It sits above both "check" and "verify." A check is quick; a verification confirms one claim. Vetting looks at the whole picture — we want the person to be who they say they are.

Vetnups follows the same logic to the letter. The second half of the name is "-nups," from nuptial — Latin for marriage. As in prenup, short for prenuptial agreement: marriage's own vetting, before the commitment.
